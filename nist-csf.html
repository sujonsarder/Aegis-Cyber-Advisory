Security Posture Assessment: NIST CSF 2.0

Current security practices at Lifeline Medical Centre evaluated across the six CSF 2.0 functions, with strengths, weaknesses and gaps.

Maturity Overview

Scored on a 1–4 scale aligned to CSF implementation tiers: 1 Partial, 2 Risk Informed, 3 Repeatable, 4 Adaptive. Target for 24 months: Tier 3 across all functions.

Overall: 1.4 / 4 (Tier 1–2). Foundational controls such as firewalls and antivirus exist, but governance, monitoring and recovery are informal and untested. Scores are the consultants' judgment from the hypothetical interview and document review.

Function-by-Function Assessment

GV · Govern Tier 1

Current practice: A short IT usage notice exists; the CIO handles security part-time. No board-level reporting or formal risk appetite.

  • Strength: Management is aware of ransomware news and supportive of improvement.
  • Weakness: No approved information security policy set, no security roles or budget line.
  • Gap: Missing policy framework, risk management strategy, supply chain oversight (GV.PO, GV.RM, GV.SC).

Justification: Without governance, technical controls are ad hoc and not tied to risk or accountability.

ID · Identify Tier 1.5

Current practice: Spreadsheet list of servers and software; no formal data classification; risk assessment never performed.

  • Strength: IT team knows the main clinical systems and their owners informally.
  • Weakness: Incomplete inventory (IoMT, shadow IT, cloud services); no vulnerability scanning.
  • Gap: No asset register with CIA ratings, risk assessment or third-party inventory (ID.AM, ID.RA).

Justification: Crown Jewels were not formally recognized, so protection is not prioritized.

PR · Protect Tier 2

Current practice: Perimeter firewall, endpoint antivirus, password login, basic user roles in HIS, annual induction briefing.

  • Strength: Firewall and antivirus deployed; server room has locked access.
  • Weakness: No MFA, flat network, shared accounts, inconsistent patching, partial encryption.
  • Gap: Missing MFA, RBAC enforcement, segmentation, encryption, security training (PR.AA, PR.DS, PR.AT, PR.PS).

Justification: Protections exist, but weak identity controls leave the Crown Jewels exposed to credential theft.

DE · Detect Tier 1

Current practice: Logs remain on individual servers; no central monitoring; incidents are noticed when users complain.

  • Strength: HIS records basic user activity.
  • Weakness: No SIEM, alerting, or review of logs; no endpoint detection.
  • Gap: Missing continuous monitoring and adverse event analysis (DE.CM, DE.AE).

Justification: Attacks could dwell for weeks undetected, increasing data theft and ransomware impact.

RS · Respond Tier 1.5

Current practice: IT staff react informally; call list kept by IT manager; no documented incident plan or regulator/patient notification process.

  • Strength: Experienced IT staff who respond quickly to outages.
  • Weakness: No defined roles, severity levels, evidence handling or communications plan.
  • Gap: Missing incident response plan, exercises and reporting (RS.MA, RS.AN, RS.CO).

Justification: Delayed, uncoordinated response magnifies patient safety and reputational impact.

RC · Recover Tier 1.5

Current practice: Daily backups to a NAS on the same network; restore never tested; no formal disaster recovery plan.

  • Strength: Backups are taken regularly and generator covers the data centre.
  • Weakness: Backups reachable by attackers; no offsite or immutable copy; unknown recovery times.
  • Gap: Missing DR plan, RTO/RPO targets, restore testing, recovery communications (RC.RP, RC.CO).

Justification: A ransomware event could destroy both production data and backups.
