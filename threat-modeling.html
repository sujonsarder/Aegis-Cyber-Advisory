Part 3 · Threat Modeling

Threat Modeling with MTMT & STRIDE

System modeling, threat identification and initial mitigation for Lifeline Medical Centre's clinical information systems.

Step 1 · System Modeling

Data flow diagram (DFD) of the major components. Rectangles are external entities, circles are processes, parallel lines are data stores, arrows are data flows and dashed boxes are trust boundaries.

External Entities

  • E1 Patients (portal / mobile app)
  • E2 Third-party vendor support
  • E3 Payment gateway and insurers
  • E4 Clinical and administrative staff

Processes & Data Stores

  • P1 Portal & API gateway, P2 VPN
  • P3 HIS/EMR, P4 LIS & PACS, P5 Pharmacy & Billing
  • D1 EMR DB, D2 PACS, D3 Billing DB
  • D4 Audit logs, D5 Backups

Trust Boundaries

  • TB1 Internet to DMZ
  • TB2 DMZ to internal clinical network
  • TB3 Internal network to data zone
  • TB4 Production data to backup storage

Step 2 & 3 · STRIDE Threat Register with Initial Mitigation

18 threats identified: 10 High, 7 Medium, 1 Low. Filter by STRIDE category.

IDSTRIDEThreat DescriptionAffected ComponentSeverityInitial Mitigation
T01SpoofingPhishing or credential theft lets an attacker impersonate a clinician and access patient records.P3 HIS/EMR, E4 workstationsHighMFA for all staff, phishing-awareness training, account lockout and conditional access.
T02SpoofingCredential stuffing against the patient portal to impersonate patients.P1 Portal & API gatewayMediumOTP/MFA for patients, rate limiting, CAPTCHA, breached-password checks.
T03SpoofingAttacker poses as a vendor to obtain remote support access.P2 VPN / remote accessHighVendor accounts with MFA, time-limited access approval, session recording, third-party agreements.
T04TamperingSQL injection or API manipulation alters or deletes patient records.P1 API, D1 EMR databaseHighParameterized queries, input validation, WAF, secure code review, penetration testing.
T05TamperingLab values or imaging results are modified in transit or at rest, leading to wrong treatment.P4 LIS/PACS, D2HighTLS for all flows, hashing/digital signatures on results, write-restricted roles, integrity monitoring.
T06TamperingRansomware encrypts or deletes backups so recovery is impossible.D5 Backup repositoryHighImmutable and offline backup copies, separate backup credentials, EDR, restore testing.
T07RepudiationA user denies changing a prescription because audit trails are incomplete.P3 HIS, P5 Pharmacy, D4MediumTamper-resistant audit logging, unique user IDs, NTP time synchronization, log retention policy.
T08RepudiationShared workstation accounts make it impossible to attribute actions to individuals.E4 staff workstationsMediumBan shared accounts, tap-in smart card SSO, acceptable use policy.
T09RepudiationPatient disputes an online payment or insurer disputes a claim submission.E3 gateway, P5 BillingLowSigned e-receipts, transaction logging, daily reconciliation with gateway.
T10Information DisclosureStaff browse records without clinical need (snooping) or excessive access exposes data.D1 EMR database, P3HighRole-based access control, break-glass with review, access monitoring, confidentiality agreements.
T11Information DisclosureSniffing of unencrypted internal traffic or rogue Wi-Fi exposes clinical data.LAN/Wi-Fi, DF7, DF9MediumTLS internally, WPA3-Enterprise, VLAN segmentation, network access control.
T12Information DisclosureBroken API authorization, lost laptops or removable media leak patient data.P1 API, staff devicesHighObject-level authorization checks, full-disk encryption, USB control, DLP, data classification.
T13Denial of ServiceDDoS attack takes the patient portal and public website offline.P1 PortalMediumCDN/DDoS protection from ISP, rate limiting, autoscaling, manual fallback for booking.
T14Denial of ServiceRansomware halts HIS, LIS and PACS, forcing paper-based care.P3, P4, serversHighEDR, network segmentation, patching, tested disaster recovery, downtime procedures.
T15Denial of ServicePower or network failure in the data centre stops all services.Infrastructure, D1–D5MediumUPS and generator, redundant links, high-availability design, environmental monitoring.
T16Elevation of PrivilegeUnpatched server vulnerabilities allow attackers to gain administrator control.P3 HIS servers, P4HighPatch management, vulnerability scanning, system hardening baselines.
T17Elevation of PrivilegeOver-privileged service and database accounts or local admin rights enable full takeover.D1, D3, E4 workstationsHighLeast privilege, privileged access management, periodic access reviews, remove local admin.
T18Elevation of PrivilegeA compromised medical device is used as a pivot into the clinical network.IoMT devices, TB3MediumDevice inventory, IoMT VLAN isolation, network access control, vendor patching agreements.
